# DOST format reference — semi-annual vs annual

A side-by-side of the two progress-report formats, taken from DOST Form 6
(*Executive Summary for the Semi-Annual Progress Report*) and DOST Form 7
(*Executive Summary for the Annual Progress Report*), each of which carries the
narrative format for its report type.

Use this to check the shipped skeletons against your funder's current forms.
**Forms change.** If yours differ, yours win — and please open an issue.

## General instructions

| | **Semi-annual (Form 6)** | **Annual (Form 7)** |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Submitted via | DPMIS | DPMIS |
| Deadline | within **one month** of each semester's end | within **two months** after each project year |
| Financial form | Form 8 — *Semi-annual* Financial Report | Form 8 — *Annual* Financial Report |
| Other attachments | Forms 11, 12 | Forms **6**, **9** (Schedule of Accounts Payable), 11, 12 |
| Printed copies | **contradictory — see below** | four (4) |
| Font | Arial 11 | Arial 11 |

> **Form 6 contradicts itself on printed copies.** Its own §I instruction says
> **four**; the narrative format embedded in the same document says **two**.
> Confirm with your funder. Four satisfies both readings.

## Preliminary pages

| # | Semi-annual | Annual |
|:--|:--|:--|
| 1 | Title page | Title page |
| 2 | Summary sheet | Summary sheet |
| 3 | List of tables, figures, illustrations, acronyms | **Acknowledgment** |
| 4 | — | **Table of contents** |
| 5 | — | List of tables, figures, illustrations, acronyms |
| 6 | — | **Abstract** — one page, single-spaced, ≤350 words |

The annual adds Acknowledgment, Table of contents, and Abstract. The semi-annual
requires none of the three.

## Text

Items 1 through 5D are **identical** in both formats apart from cosmetics
(`5A` vs `5a`, "refers to" vs "list").

| Item | Both formats |
|:--|:--|
| 1 | Introduction |
| 2 | Review of literature — (a) related research and current technologies, (b) scientific/technical merit, (c) the project leader's related research, (d) prior art search, (e) other relevant materials |
| 3 | Scientific Basis / Theoretical Framework |
| 4 | Methodology — (a) variables and parameters, (b) treatments and layout, (c) experimental procedures and design, (d) statistical analysis, (e) evaluation method and observations; plus strategies for implementation |
| 5A/5a | Discussion of Result and Findings — including the comparison of targeted against actual output |
| 5B/5b | Outputs (6Ps) |
| 5C/5c | Outcomes |
| 5D/5d | Potential Impacts (2Is) |

Then the two diverge:

| Semi-annual | Annual |
|:--|:--|
| 6 Literature Cited | 6 **Summary and conclusion** |
| 7 Appendices | 7 **Recommendation for future R&D work** (6 categories) |
| 8 Problems | then Literature Cited |
| 9 Raw data | then Appendices |
| 10 Attachments | then **Terminal Audited Financial Report** |
| | then **List of Personnel Involved** |
| | then **List of Equipment** |
| | then Problems, Raw data, Attachments |

The annual's six recommendation categories: technologies needing further
verification; technologies for commercialization and dissemination; R&D
breakthrough; formulated solution to a specific problem; policy formulation;
policy implementation and adoption.

> Form 7's own numbering restarts partway through its back matter — the source
> document's list formatting is inconsistent. The order above follows the
> sequence it presents.

## The 6Ps

Both forms define them identically, in the Operational Definition of Terms:

> deliverables of the project based on the 6Ps metrics (Publication,
> Patent/Intellectual Property, Product, People Service, Place and Partnership,
> and Policy)

and both carry a six-row table: Publications, Patents/IP, Products, People
Services, Places and Partnerships, Policy.

**They are six.** The narrative format's prose — "publications, patents filed,
products developed, facilities and partnerships established, people trained and
graduated, public service provided and science-based policies" — *reads* as seven
because "people trained and graduated" and "public service provided" are two
examples of one P, People Service.

Some projects report "7Ps" and have been accepted. If yours does, treat it as a
declared deviation rather than as the format's own wording.

## Appendices

Both formats require the same four:

1. Statistical formula and analysis
2. Sample questionnaire / survey form / interview schedule
3. Report of income generated
4. Proof of 6Ps

## The escape clause

Both formats close with the same note:

> For non-R&D projects, items that are not applicable do not need to be provided.

This template's position, per Constitution Principle II: **keep the heading and
state non-applicability with the reason.** A deleted heading is
indistinguishable from an oversight; a heading saying "not applicable, because…"
is visibly a decision.

## Executive-summary forms

Forms 6 and 7 are also the executive-summary forms themselves, filled in and
submitted alongside the narrative report. They are **not** produced by this
repository.

| | Form 6 | Form 7 |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Numbered items | (1)–(9) | (1)–(13) |
| Risk Management Plan | absent | **present** — item (8), with Risks and Assumptions, Strategies, Actions to be Taken |
| Problems/Concerns | item (8) | item (12) |
| Suggested solutions | item (9) | item (13) |
| Accomplishments table | percentage split into **For the period** and **Cumulative (from start)** | single percentage column |

The semi-annual form asks for cumulative tracking that the annual does not —
counterintuitive, but that is what the forms say.
